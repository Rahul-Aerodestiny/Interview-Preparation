📘 Basic Aerospace Interview Questions

Essential questions and answers for Aerospace Engineering interviews.

1. Explain Bernoulli’s principle in aerodynamics.

Ans: It states that an increase in fluid velocity decreases pressure. In wings, faster air on top creates lift.

2. What is the difference between laminar and turbulent flow?

Ans: Laminar flow has smooth, orderly motion while turbulent flow is chaotic, with eddies and vortices.

3. Define Mach number and its significance.

Ans: Mach = aircraft speed / speed of sound. It determines subsonic, transonic, supersonic, hypersonic regimes.

4. What are the primary flight control surfaces?

Ans: Ailerons (roll), Elevators (pitch), Rudder (yaw).

5. What is flutter in aerospace structures?

Ans: Flutter is a self-excited oscillation caused by aerodynamic and structural interaction, potentially destructive.

6. Explain stress vs strain in materials.

Ans: Stress = force per unit area, strain = deformation per unit length. They define material behavior under loads.

7. What are composite materials, and why are they used in aerospace?

Ans: Composites (e.g., CFRP) are lightweight, strong, fatigue-resistant, improving efficiency and fuel savings.

8. Difference between jet engines and rocket engines?

Ans: Jet engines require atmospheric oxygen, rockets carry oxidizer onboard and can work in space.

9. What is the Reynolds number?

Ans: A dimensionless number (Re = ρVL/μ) predicting laminar/turbulent flow transition.

10. Explain the concept of boundary layer.

Ans: The thin layer of fluid near a surface where viscous effects are significant, crucial for drag and heat transfer.

11. What are canards in aircraft design?

Ans: Small forward wings ahead of the main wing, improving lift, maneuverability, and stall control.

12. Explain fatigue failure in aerospace structures.

Ans: Failure under repeated cyclic loading, even below yield stress, due to crack initiation and propagation.

13. What is the purpose of wind tunnel testing?

Ans: To simulate airflow around models, study lift, drag, stability, and validate CFD results.

14. Differentiate between subsonic, supersonic, and hypersonic flows.

Ans: Subsonic: M<1, Supersonic: M>1, Hypersonic: M>5. Each has distinct aerodynamic phenomena.

15. What is specific impulse in propulsion?

Ans: Measure of efficiency of rocket/jet engines, defined as thrust per unit mass flow of propellant.

16. Explain difference between static stability and dynamic stability.

Ans: Static: initial tendency after disturbance. Dynamic: time history of motion as it returns to equilibrium.

17. Why is titanium used in aerospace?

Ans: High strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resistance, and ability to withstand high temperatures.

18. What is escape velocity?

Ans: The minimum velocity required for an object to escape Earth’s gravity without propulsion, ~11.2 km/s.

19. Explain aspect ratio of a wing.

Ans: Ratio of wingspan² to wing area. High aspect ratio = efficient lift, low induced drag.

20. What is the difference between UAVs and manned aircraft design considerations?

Ans: UAVs focus on endurance, autonomy, lightweight materials, while manned aircraft focus on safety, comfort, and redundancy.
